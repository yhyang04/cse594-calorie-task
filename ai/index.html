Calorie Log CheckWith AI

Is the app's calorie log right?

This is a short study for CSE 594 (about 8–9 minutes, 7 meals). You will look at photos of real cafeteria meals and judge whether a food-diary app logged their calories correctly, with help from an AI assistant.

Your answers are stored on a secure server and used only for this class assignment. Your uniqname is used only to link your two sessions (with and without AI) and is replaced by an anonymous ID in the analysis. You can stop at any time.

Please use the same uniqname in both conditions.

Instructions

Food-diary apps can log a meal's calories automatically, but the logged value is sometimes wrong. For each meal you will see an overhead photo and the calories the app logged. Your job:

  1. Decide whether the logged value is About right (within ±25 % of the meal's true calories), Too high (more than 25 % above), or Too low (more than 25 % below).
  2. Type your own estimate of the meal's calories (a rough number is fine).
  3. Say how confident you are.

The true calories were measured by weighing every ingredient (Nutrition5k dataset). Photos are taken from directly above a single serving.

Example

The app logged 530 kcal for this steak. The steak actually has about 320 kcal.

530 is more than 25 % above 320 (the upper limit would be about 400), so the correct answer is Too high.

Calorie cheat sheet (also available during the task)
  • 1 cup cooked rice or pasta (~160 g) ≈ 200 kcal
  • 100 g grilled chicken breast ≈ 165 kcal · 100 g steak ≈ 250 kcal
  • 1 large egg ≈ 75 kcal · 1 slice of pizza (~100 g) ≈ 270 kcal
  • 1 tbsp oil or butter ≈ 100–120 kcal · 2 tbsp dressing ≈ 100–150 kcal
  • A handful (28 g) of nuts ≈ 165 kcal · 1 cup salad greens ≈ 10 kcal
  • 1 medium apple ≈ 95 kcal · 1 cup berries ≈ 70 kcal

Your AI assistant

For every meal you will first give your own answer. Then an AI assistant analyses the photo live and you can keep or change your answer.

  • Detailed AI (Google Gemini vision model) lists the foods it sees with estimated grams and calories.
  • Second-opinion AI is a small image-only model trained on ~2,700 other cafeteria meals; it gives only a total.
  • Neither AI sees the logged value. Each AI's verdict compares the logged value with its own estimate using the same ±25 % rule.
  • In our tests each AI's verdict was right about 7 out of 10 times. It most often misjudges portion sizes and hidden calories (oil, sauce, cheese).
  • If you think the AI got something wrong (e.g. "the rice portion is twice as big"), you can tell it and it will re-estimate.
